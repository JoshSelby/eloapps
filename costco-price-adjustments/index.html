How Costco price adjustments work

If Costco lowers the price of something you bought within the last 30 days, you can usually get the difference back. Here's how, and what doesn't qualify.

The 30-day rule

Costco refunds the difference when an item's price drops within 30 days of your purchase. The most common case is an item showing up in the monthly coupon book (instant savings) a week or two after you bought it at full price.

Example: you buy laundry detergent for $19.99, and ten days later it's $4 off in the coupon book. Within 30 days of your purchase, you can ask for that $4 back, plus the tax on it.

How to ask for one

Warehouse purchases:

  1. Note the item number on the shelf tag or in the coupon book.
  2. Go to the Member Services counter (where returns are handled).
  3. They look up your purchase with your membership card, so you usually don't need the paper receipt. The difference goes back to your original payment method.

Costco.com orders: request the adjustment through Costco.com (price adjustment request or customer service) within 30 days of the order.

What doesn't qualify

  • The other channel's price. A warehouse purchase is adjusted to the warehouse price, and an online order to the online price. You can't mix them.
  • Purchases older than 30 days. The window is strict.
  • Some clearance and special items. Some clearance markdowns, limited promotions and items like gold bullion may be excluded. Member Services can tell you.

Finding adjustments you missed

The hard part isn't asking. It's noticing. You'd have to remember what you bought, then check every coupon book and price change for 30 days.

Receipt Insights does that from the receipts in your Costco account:

  • Matches this month's coupon book against what you bought in the last 30 days at full price, with a claim-by date for each item
  • Spots items you bought again on sale within 30 days without getting an adjustment
  • Lists every adjustment you've already received
